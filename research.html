Research

My research focuses on the theory and applications of time-varying photonic systems, including active and time-varying metasurfaces, photonic time crystals, and their interactions with classical and quantum light. I develop analytical and computational models to understand and control dynamic light–matter interactions.

My earlier work investigated tunable metamaterial absorbers for sensing and the optical manipulation of nanoparticles. More recently, I have studied time-modulated particles and metasurfaces, including their scattering properties and their interactions with quantum emitters. This work combines electromagnetic modeling with approaches such as time-Floquet analysis, multipole methods, and open quantum-system dynamics.

My current research interests include programmable quantum-light emission, electrically tunable photonic materials, and photonic time crystals. Across these directions, I am interested in how temporal modulation and engineered material responses can enable new ways to shape, steer, and control light.
